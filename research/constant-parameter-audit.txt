Universal constants and parameter-dependent bounds — audit, 2026-11-16

PODC 2016: Amiri, Schmid and Siebertz
https://schmiste.github.io/podc16.pdf#page=6
Theorem 15 gives 24g + O(1) approximation and 12g + O(1) rounds. The catalog now states these explicit bounds on orientable-genus-at-most-g inputs, rather than collapsing either to O(1). The earlier abstract-level record hid both dependencies.

TALG extension: locally embeddable graphs
https://schmiste.github.io/talg19ds.pdf#page=11
Algorithm 1 and Lemma 3.8 give 6c^2 t + (2t+5)c + 4, where c bounds depth-one minor edge density and K_{3,t} is excluded as a depth-one minor. c changes a local-computation threshold; t appears in the analysis. The LOCAL communication phases have fixed radii and a universal count. Theorem 3.9 independently states a universal constant-round O(g^2) genus algorithm; the preprocessing algorithm in Theorem 3.11 has a different, genus-dependent round count. Do not infer runtime dependence from the approximation factor alone.

Kublenz–Siebertz–Vigny, corrected v3
https://arxiv.org/html/2012.02701v3#S6
The approximation depends on depth-one density (Theorem 1.1), while Section 6 implements a fixed number of bounded-radius LOCAL exchanges. The catalog shows f(depth-one density) for the factor and Constant for the rounds.

Heydt et al. 2022
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.02669#page=5
Theorem 2.1 and its following discussion explicitly retain depth-one-density dependence in the factor and runtime. Both fields now show f(depth-one density).

Amiri et al. distance domination, 2017/2018
https://arxiv.org/html/1702.02848v2#S4
The distance-r and connected-distance-r approximation factors depend on the expansion function and r. The catalog preserves the stated O(r^2 log n) bound with the fixed-class assumptions, and makes the factor's dependencies visible. These are separate papers from PODC 2016.

Other coarse factors
The excluded-topological-minor factor depends on h. The four high-girth distance-cover factors depend on radius and expansion. Their raw O(1) statements remain in source fields, while their displayed factors retain these parameters.

Lower bounds
https://jukkasuomela.fi/doc/linear-order.pdf#page=4
https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09618v2#S8.SS4
https://timothepicavet.fr/others/thesis.pdf#page=146
The GHS edge-domination bound permits arbitrary degree-dependent locality. The path-power results permit arbitrary functions of their fixed power/radius and approximation-gap parameters. The thesis minor/pathwidth result excludes n-independent round bounds after fixing H and epsilon. These are excluded budgets, not achieved runtimes or fabricated Omega functions. Named lower budgets explicitly say “rounds ruled out”. The high-girth sublinear lower bound also retains radius and expansion-bound dependence.

Implementation and SOTA
Approximation and runtime dependencies are independently curated in round-labels.json. Bare O(1) fields require a field-specific universalConstants review; otherwise the build rejects a new result. Named and explicit variable n-independent round bounds enter the parameter regime, immediately after universal constants. Source formulas, original regimes and semantic parameter metadata remain available in data/CSV exports. Reduction substitutions preserve both fields independently.

No historical Amiri record was marked SOTA before this audit; the misleading Constant approximation labels and shared regime were the primary problems. Seventeen SOTA descriptions now state the full parameter dependence and distinguish optimal coefficients or fixed-parameter n-dependence from unknown joint parameter optimality. Dominance descriptions distinguish universal round bounds from f(epsilon). The planar a=3 certificate remains valid because it checks the same protocol and fixed solver parameters, rather than assuming arbitrary parameter functions equal a universal constant.

Correction to the earlier runtime classification: the genus and linkless
outside-k-domination procedures in ICTCS2017 use at most two universal
communication rounds. Their genus/multiplicity parameters affect thresholds
and approximation factors, not the number of broadcasts. The factors retain
their parameter dependence. Do not infer runtime dependence from thresholds.

Counts reconstructed from communication phases use the ≤ sign and an explicit
schedule in the result details. They are not optimality or minimum-round
claims. Source O(1) expressions remain unchanged in the original fields.
Unrelated unspecified constants cannot suppress an audited numerical budget.
The universal lower-bound budgets are not replaced by fixed algorithm counts.
